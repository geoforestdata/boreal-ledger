Boreal recovery after disturbance

Wildfire and timber harvest alter boreal forests in different ways. Using matched disturbance events and nearby reference forests, this study asks whether their satellite-observed trajectories converge over time.

View repository

Study landscape

One managed boreal landscape, two disturbance pathways.

The final design uses official disturbance records and a 100 km study extent near Lebel-sur-Quevillon. The public map shows only selected analytical events retained in the matched sampling frame, not the full forestry inventory.

Matched design

Recovery needs a reference.

Each wildfire event is matched with a harvest event from the same disturbance year. Disturbed forest sites are then compared with nearby screened reference forest, keeping the event as the inferential unit.

The core Landsat metric is the reference-relative gap: NBR gap = reference NBR - disturbed NBR. A shrinking gap indicates increasing spectral similarity to the matched reference forest, not complete ecological recovery.

Final design: 76 wildfire events, 76 harvest events, and 76 exact-year fire-harvest event pairs.

Main result · Landsat

Most NBR convergence occurs during roughly the first decade.

Wildfire produces a substantially larger early post-disturbance NBR departure from matched reference forest than harvest. Recovery is nonlinear, and both pathways approach reference conditions at later ages, with greater uncertainty as event support declines.

Different satellite representations

Apparent recovery depends on what aspect of the satellite signal is observed.

Landsat NBR, Sentinel-2 multiband spectral distance, and AlphaEarth representation-space distance do not measure the same thing. Their trajectories are compared as shapes, not as interchangeable units.

Sentinel-2

Multiband distance shows a related but non-identical pattern.

Sentinel-2 standardized six-band distance is elevated at early ages and later declines, indicating increased multiband similarity to matched reference forest. Fire and harvest are not consistently ordered across age.

AlphaEarth

Representation-space distance does not simply mirror NBR.

AlphaEarth remains elevated at intermediate ages, especially for wildfire, even where NBR has substantially converged. This is a satellite representation-space result.

AlphaEarth distance is not a direct measure of biomass, biodiversity, stand structure, ecosystem function, or complete ecological recovery.

Synthesis

A forest can become spectrally similar to its reference without becoming indistinguishable in every satellite representation.

What the study supports

  • Nonlinear reference-relative NBR convergence.
  • Larger early wildfire NBR departure from reference forest.
  • Later convergence in conventional spectral information.
  • Non-identical multivariate and representation-space trajectories.
  • Persistent AlphaEarth representation-space separation at some later ages.

What it does not infer

  • Biomass recovery.
  • Biodiversity recovery.
  • Ecosystem function recovery.
  • Complete ecological recovery.
  • Causal superiority of wildfire or harvest.
  • Universal AlphaEarth superiority.

Key limitation: Sentinel-2 and AlphaEarth observations span 2017-2025, so older apparent recovery ages combine within-event temporal change with cross-cohort comparisons.

Methods

Concise methods, full reproducibility in code.

The public page emphasizes the scientific story. The repository contains the complete reproducible workflow and derived outputs.

Study area and disturbance data

The study extent was selected near Lebel-sur-Quevillon, Quebec, using official disturbance histories and temporal support. Wildfire comes from mapped fire records; harvest is represented by official forestry intervention records in the retained workflow.

Event construction and matching

Fire and harvest events were screened, sampled, and matched by exact disturbance year. The inferential unit is the disturbance event, not the individual site-year.

Reference forest selection

Reference forests were selected near disturbed sites after screening for disturbance and environmental similarity. The final reference table is preserved as a GeoPackage output.

analysis/08_select_and_extract_reference_timeseries.py
Landsat recovery model

Landsat NBR gap change was modelled at event-year level with nonlinear age-associated trajectories and event-aware uncertainty.

Sentinel-2 and AlphaEarth

Sentinel-2 uses standardized six-band disturbed-reference distance. AlphaEarth uses annual 64-dimensional representation distance. Both are interpreted as satellite-signal similarity, not direct ecological state.

Limitations

The design is observational. Older Sentinel-2 and AlphaEarth ages mix within-event temporal change with cohort substitution. A shrinking satellite gap is evidence of increasing similarity to a matched reference signal, not full recovery of forest structure or function.
