Boreal Ledger

Can disturbance history still be seen 19 years later?

Two nearby boreal forest sites lost canopy in 2005. One was associated with wildfire. The other with probable harvest. In 2024, can satellite data still tell them apart?

Central result

Conventional spectra struggle. AlphaEarth separates more.

The 19-year test uses 222 geographically matched fire-harvest pairs, a median pair distance of 0.160 km, a 90 m fire-interior exclusion, and 5 km spatial-block cross-validation.

Why geography matters

Nearby pairs reduce broad geographic confounding.

The comparison does not let a large fire patch simply overwhelm the model. Fire and probable-harvest samples are matched one to one, and each pair is assigned to the same spatial block.

Other ages

The project does not claim universal AlphaEarth superiority.

1 year / 2023

AlphaEarth advantage is modest, so this is not emphasized.

12 years / 2012

Promising, but fewer matched pairs and Sentinel-2 becomes competitive under stricter buffers.

19 years / 2005

Strongest public case after geographic matching and fire-edge controls.

Supported

AlphaEarth embeddings retain greater wildfire-probable-harvest separability than conventional spectral baselines for the older matched class in this study landscape.

Not supported

  • AlphaEarth detects old fires.
  • AlphaEarth measures ecosystem recovery directly.
  • AlphaEarth measures biomass.
  • The comparison proves causal effects.
  • The result generalizes automatically beyond this study landscape.
